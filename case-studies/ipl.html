Who watches the IPL, and how they watch

Before redesigning a single screen, we needed to understand who actually opens the IPL app, and when. Interviews, app-store review mining and a two-week diary study across six cities showed a fanbase that ranges from a five-minute score-check between meetings to a four-hour second-screen ritual during a final, all inside the same product.

Three fans, three very different needs

Engagement research kept surfacing the same tension: a single home screen was trying to serve a quick glance, a deep dive and a social moment all at once. We anchored the redesign around three archetypes that captured the spread.

AR

The Commuter Fan

Arjun, 27 · catches overs between metro stops

"I just need to know the score and get on with my day, I don't have three minutes to wait for a video to load."

  • Instant score glance, no login wall
  • Works reliably on patchy network
  • Push alerts only for his team's matches
VK

The Die-Hard Fan

Vikram, 34 · full-time analyst, part-time everything else

"Give me every ball, every stat, every player number, and let me set my fantasy team without leaving the app."

  • Ball-by-ball commentary with filters
  • Deep player and team statistics
  • Fantasy team management in-app
SN

The Social Viewer

Sanya, 23 · watches for the vibe, stays for the banter

"Half the fun is reacting with my group chat the second something wild happens on screen."

  • Shareable highlight and moment cards
  • Live polls and predictions
  • Lightweight reactions during play

Mapping persona pain to design decisions

Every research finding was traced to a concrete interface decision, so nothing in the final design existed without a reason a fan had given us.

Fans lose live score context the moment they switch apps to check anything else
→
A persistent mini score bar pinned across every screen in the app
Commuters on patchy network abandon video-heavy screens mid-load
→
A lightweight, text-first Match Center with an adaptive low-data mode
Die-hard fans found ball-by-ball detail buried under casual highlight content
→
A dedicated, filterable ball-by-ball commentary feed of its own
Fantasy players had to leave the app to check live player form before setting a team
→
In-line player stat cards built directly into the Fantasy team-builder
Social viewers had no quick way to react or share a moment mid-match
→
One-tap reactions and shareable clip cards inside the Live Match Center
New fans found five flat tabs (News, Videos, Scores, Fantasy, Shop) hard to parse
→
Four core tabs restructured around live match state, personalised by fan type

The screens, mapped to fan needs

Each surface was designed to answer one persona's core question fast, while staying one tap away from the others, so a commuter's quick glance and a die-hard's deep dive live comfortably in the same app.

Results after one IPL season

Shipped ahead of the following season, the redesign was measured against the same behavioural benchmarks that shaped it, speed to score, depth of engagement, and return frequency.

One score bar, every screen

Persisting live state across the whole app, not just the Match Center, removed the single biggest source of drop-off.

Depth should be optional, not default

Separating casual and deep-dive layers let both personas feel at home without either one feeling underserved.

Design for the second screen

With 40% of fans watching TV and app together, the app's job during play is to complement the broadcast, not compete with it.
